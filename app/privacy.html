Privacy

Leanward, version in development · last updated Nov 3, 2026

What this early version stores

Your account. You sign in with Google. Google tells Leanward your name, email address, profile picture link and an account ID, and Leanward's database (Supabase) keeps them so you can sign in again. Leanward never sees your Google password and gets no access to your Gmail, contacts or files.

What you enter when you set up. Your date of birth, the sex used for the calorie formula (or "prefer not to say"), height, weight, activity level and training sessions a week; if you choose to, your body fat (and how it was measured), your waist and whether you're pregnant or breastfeeding; your goal, phase, targets and food preferences. These are used only to work out and explain your targets.

Your measurements. Weigh-ins (weight, day, time and any note) and tape measurements such as waist. They're used to draw your trend and charts and to tell you whether your phase is working.

Your diary and your own foods. What you log (food, amount, meal, day and the nutrients for that amount), foods you create or copy, meals you save, your meal names and order, and any "Report a problem" you send about a food.

Where it's kept. On your phone, and backed up to your own rows in Leanward's database (Supabase), which only you can read in the app; other users can't see them. Leanward's owner, as the database's administrator, can technically view the database, but only looks at an account's data when you ask for help with it. Data travels encrypted (HTTPS). Foods you create are private: they never join the shared catalog.

Food search and barcodes. Searching foods asks Leanward's own database. When you scan a barcode that isn't in it, the app asks Open Food Facts (openfoodfacts.org), a public food database, for that one barcode. Only the barcode number is sent: nothing about you, your diary or your account. If you tap Search online, the words you typed in the search box are sent to Open Food Facts to find matching products, again with nothing about you. Open Food Facts may log that request like any website does. The camera is only on while the scanner is open, and the picture never leaves your phone.

Error reports. If the app hits a bug, it sends a short technical report to Leanward's own database: a masked error message (quoted text and numbers removed), which file and line failed, the app version and your browser's name and version. It never includes food, weight, measurements or anything you typed. No other company receives these reports.

On your phone. A copy of the app's files is kept so it opens offline, plus your sign-in (so you stay signed in), your name and email, your last-opened tab, and the details above. Signing out removes all of it from the phone; your backup stays in your account.

What's planned

There will be no ads, and health or nutrition data will never be sold or shared. Exporting your data and deleting your account from inside the app are coming (ACC-1); until then, ask the owner to delete your account and everything in it. This page will be updated before anything new is stored.

Food data

Most foods come from USDA FoodData Central (public domain). Foods marked Community come from Open Food Facts, © Open Food Facts contributors, available under the Open Database License (ODbL); they're crowd-sourced, so check them against the label. Barcode scanning uses ZXing (Apache License 2.0).
